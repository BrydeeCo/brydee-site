How We Handle Gluten in Our Shared Kitchen

An honest look at how our gluten-free treats are made, and who they are right for.

Home › Blog › How We Handle Gluten in Our Shared Kitchen

By Brydee, founder & baker · 2026-07-08

When you avoid gluten, 'gluten-free' is about more than the recipe – it is also about how and where the food is made. We think you deserve a straight answer, so here is exactly how we do things at Brydee & Co.

We bake in a shared home kitchen

Brydee & Co is a home-based bakery. Our kitchen is not a separate, gluten-free-only facility – the same kitchen is sometimes used for baking that does contain gluten, wheat and dairy. We are upfront about that, because it is the honest picture and it helps you decide what is right for you.

How we keep our gluten-free bakes gluten-free

Every gluten-free batch is made to a gluten-free recipe, with no gluten-containing ingredients, and we follow a strict routine to keep it that way:

  • Gluten-free ingredients are stored separately and sealed, away from any wheat flour.
  • Benches, boards, bowls and equipment are cleaned down thoroughly before we start a gluten-free bake.
  • We prep and bake gluten-free items separately, using clean utensils and bakeware.
  • We plan our baking so gluten-free treats are made with a fresh, clean setup, not alongside anything containing gluten.

What 'gluten-free' means for us

It means our recipes contain no gluten. Our treats are made for people who choose to avoid gluten, or who are sensitive or intolerant to it, and who want a genuinely good cookie without the gluten. Because we bake in a shared home kitchen, we cannot promise zero traces of gluten, so we do not recommend our food for anyone with coeliac disease or a diagnosed wheat or gluten allergy. If that is you, we would rather be honest than risk your health.

Have a question about how something is made? Get in touch before you order – we are always happy to talk you through it.

Frequently asked

Is your kitchen a dedicated gluten-free kitchen?
No. We are a home-based bakery and the same kitchen is sometimes used for baking that contains gluten. We keep our gluten-free bakes gluten-free by storing ingredients separately and cleaning down thoroughly before every gluten-free bake.
Can people with coeliac disease eat your treats?
Because we bake in a shared kitchen we can't guarantee against traces of gluten, so we don't recommend our treats for coeliac disease or a diagnosed gluten or wheat allergy. They are made for people avoiding gluten by choice or intolerance.
How do you avoid cross-contamination?
We store gluten-free ingredients separately, clean down all benches and equipment before a gluten-free bake, and use clean, separate utensils and bakeware. It minimises the risk, though in a shared kitchen it cannot be ruled out entirely.
